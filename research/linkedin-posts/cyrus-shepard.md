## Post 1
**Date:** June 10, 2026

Our latest study of Google's Core Update shows a clear pattern:

Sites that relied heavily on programmatic, unedited AI content to target long-tail keywords lost an average of 40% of their organic traffic.
Meanwhile, sites that used AI to optimize their existing, human-written content (adding semantic entities, clarifying structure, updating out-of-date facts) saw a 15% increase in visibility.

Google's Helpful Content System is actively filtering out "AI clutter."
If you're using AI to write *new* pages, you're taking a risk. If you're using AI to *improve* your best existing pages, you're winning.

Focus on refinement, not creation.

## Post 2
**Date:** May 19, 2026

What makes content "helpful" according to Google?

It's not word count. It's not keyword density.
It's E-E-A-T (Experience, Expertise, Authoritativeness, and Trustworthiness).
And the hardest one of those to fake with AI is **Experience**.

Does your page contain:
- First-person pronouns ("we tested," "in my experience")?
- Original photos or videos of you actually using the product or performing the service?
- Unique data or survey results you gathered yourself?

If the answer is no, Google will classify your content as low-effort, regardless of whether a human or AI wrote it.

Insert real experience into every page.

## Post 3
**Date:** April 16, 2026

With the rollout of Google's AI Overviews, many SEOs are panicking about the drop in clicks.

But here's the silver lining:
AI Overviews cite their sources. And those citations are highly targeted.

We analyzed 1,000 AI Overviews and found that Google favors pages that answer the user's query in a clear, structured format (e.g., tables, bullet points) right at the top of the page.
If you make it easy for Google's model to extract the answer from your page, you will get the citation.

Format your pages for extraction, and you'll capture the AI search traffic.
