Do LLMs and AI Search Engines change how we optimize pages?

The short answer: No. The mathematical fundamentals of on-page SEO remain exactly the same.

We recently ran an experiment across 50 domains testing how Google and LLMs (like Perplexity and ChatGPT Search) extract entities from a page.
The results: Both types of algorithms rely on structured mathematical signals.

If your target search query is placed in the H1, URL, and title tag, and supported by a mathematically optimized keyword density (which we calculate in PageOptimizer Pro), your page is far more likely to rank in Google AND get cited as a primary source in AI Overviews.

Stop treating AI SEO like it's magic. It's math. Optimize the signals, and the traffic will follow.

## Post 2
**Date:** May 12, 2026

One of the biggest myths in SEO is that Google has an "AI content detector" that automatically penalizes AI-generated text.

It doesn't. And it won't.

We've run multiple single-variable tests ranking pages written 100% by AI (using Claude and GPT models) against human-written content.
As long as the AI content is mathematically optimized for the target query—meaning the terms, entities, and structure match the top ranking pages—it ranks.

Google's algorithm doesn't care who wrote the text. It cares about whether the page satisfies the search intent and contains the correct contextual signals.

Instead of trying to make your AI content "sound human," focus on making it scientifically correct for the query.
