## Post 1
**Date:** June 4, 2026

We just ran a benchmark test comparing GPT, Claude, and Gemini models for generating SEO content at agency scale.

We generated 10 articles with each model, using the same detailed outline and prompt templates.
Here is what we found:

- **Claude Sonnet** won on tone, readability, and structural flow. It sounded the least "AI-ish" and handled complex formatting beautifully.
- **GPT models** were the fastest and cheapest, but required the most editing because of repetitive phrasing ("delve," "testament," "realm").
- **Gemini Pro** was excellent at integrating real-world Google search context, but the prose was sometimes dry.

Our takeaway: We are building our custom workflows to use Claude for drafting and Gemini for real-time fact-checking and entity matching.

## Post 2
**Date:** May 16, 2026

Stop using AI to generate the entire article in one go.
That's how you get generic, repetitive paragraphs that add zero value.

Instead, break down the production into a multi-step workflow:

1. **Research & Structure:** Use AI to analyze the top 10 search results and suggest an outline of H2s/H3s based on competitor gaps.
2. **Section-by-Section Drafting:** Have the AI write one section at a time, feeding it specific source materials (interviews, data) for each section.
3. **Internal Linking & Entity Insertion:** Use a separate model to review the draft and suggest internal links and semantic entity matches.
4. **Human Editorial Review:** Pass the draft to a human editor to verify facts, polish the tone, and add unique brand voice.

It takes longer, but the quality difference is massive.

## Post 3
**Date:** April 12, 2026

The real cost of AI content isn't the API fees. It's the editing.

If you think AI is a magic money printer that eliminates content costs, think again.

When we transition clients to AI-assisted content production, we actually end up hiring *more* senior editors.
A junior writer can be replaced by a good prompt. But you need a highly skilled editor to take an AI draft, verify the claims, strip out the fluff, insert proprietary insights, and make it actually worth reading.

If you aren't investing in editing, you're just publishing clutter that Google will ignore.
