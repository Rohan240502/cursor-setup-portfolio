## Post 1
**Date:** June 6, 2026

We just transitioned our agency's entire keyword research process to an AI-assisted model.

Here is the step-by-step workflow:
1. **Raw Export:** Pull search terms and competitor data from Ahrefs or Semrush.
2. **Intent Clustering:** Feed the data to Claude to group the keywords by intent (e.g., informational, transactional, brand).
3. **Question Identification:** Have the AI extract the specific questions users ask in search results related to these clusters.
4. **Content Plan:** Map out which questions need dedicated articles and which should be addressed in existing content.

It used to take our strategists two full days to build a 30-day content plan. Now it takes 3 hours, and the clustering is actually more accurate.

## Post 2
**Date:** May 22, 2026

The biggest bottleneck in AI content creation is standardizing the formatting and brand tone.

If you don't give the AI a style guide, it will sound like every other chatbot: using emojis, bullet points, and passive voice.
We built a custom style guide for our agency that specifies:

- Active voice only.
- Limit paragraph length to 3 sentences max.
- Avoid clichés like "in conclusion" or "it's important to remember."
- Format lists with markdown checkboxes instead of bullets.

Now, 90% of our AI-generated drafts match our brand guidelines on the first pass, cutting down edit times by half.

## Post 3
**Date:** April 14, 2026

Are you optimization-obsessed or user-obsessed?

Lately, I've seen too many SEOs use AI tools to stuff semantic entities into articles to get a high on-page optimization score.
The result is unreadable paragraphs that read like they were written by a robot.

Yes, semantic optimization is important.
But if the reader bounces because the content is hard to read, Google's user engagement signals will eventually tank your rankings anyway.

Optimize for the user first, and use AI to check the entities later.
